## Question 3: A shop sells 1 kg of mangoes for ฿50. What is the cost of 0.1 kg? (MULTIPLE_CHOICE)

**Explanation / Steps:**
1. A shop sells 1 kg of mangoes for ฿50.

3. Multiply the price by 0.1: \(50 × 0.1 = 5\).  
4. So 0.1 kg costs ฿5.

## Question 5: 0.25 × 0.01 = ? (MULTIPLE_CHOICE)
**Correct Answer:** 0.0025  

**Explanation / Steps:**
1. Write each number without its decimal: 0.25 → 25, 0.01 → 1.  
2. Multiply the whole numbers: 25 × 1 = 25.  
3. Count the total number of decimal places in the original numbers (2 in 0.25 + 2 in 0.01 = 4).  
4. Put the decimal point four places from the right side of the result: 25 → 0.0025.  

**Feedback**  
Excellent! Most of you got the answer right. Keep up the good work!

**Common Mistakes**  
- Putting the decimal only one place too far left, giving 0.025.  
- Forgetting to count all four decimal places when shifting the decimal back.  

**Keep Practicing**  
Try more problems like 0.3 × 0.04 or 0.07 × 0.02. Focus on counting the decimal places before you move the decimal point.

## Question 10: Multiplying by 0.1 makes a number 10 times smaller. (MULTIPLE_CHOICE)
**Correct Answer:** Correct  

**Explanation / Steps:**  
1. 0.1 means “one‑tenth.”  
2. When you multiply a number by 0.1, you move the decimal point one place to the left.  
3. Moving the decimal left is the same as dividing the number by 10.  
4. Dividing by 10 makes the number ten times smaller.  
5. Therefore, multiplying by 0.1 makes a number 10 times smaller.  

## Feedback  
Excellent work! Most of you answered correctly and understood the idea of moving the decimal point. Keep up the good effort!  

## Common Mistakes  
1. **Not moving the decimal point** – some students left the decimal in the same place.  
2. **Thinking 0.1 is zero** – forgetting that 0.1 is a small number, not 0.  
3. **Confusing multiplication with division** – mixing up the direction of the decimal shift.  

## Keep Practicing  
Try multiplying different numbers by 0.1 and 0.01 to see how many places the decimal moves. Practice moving the decimal point to reinforce the rule.

## Question 11: Dividing by 0.01 makes a number smaller. (MULTIPLE_CHOICE)

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing “divide by 0.01” with “divide by 100.”  
- Thinking that dividing by a small number always makes the result smaller.

## Keep Practicing  
Try a few problems where you divide by fractions like 0.05 or 0.2; notice how the result changes. It’s a handy trick to remember!
