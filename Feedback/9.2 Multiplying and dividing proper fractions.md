## Question 5: Simplify 4/6 × 1/1. (MULTIPLE_CHOICE)
**Correct Answer:** 2/3  

**Explanation / Steps:**  
1. Multiply the numerators: \(4 \times 1 = 4\).  
2. Multiply the denominators: \(6 \times 1 = 6\).  
3. Simplify the fraction \(4/6\).  
   - Find the greatest common divisor of 4 and 6, which is 2.  
   - Divide both numerator and denominator by 2:  
     \(4 ÷ 2 = 2\), \(6 ÷ 2 = 3\).  
4. The simplified result is \(2/3\).

## Feedback

## Question 6: Calculate 1 ÷ 1/2. (MULTIPLE_CHOICE)
**Correct Answer:** 2  

**Explanation / Steps:**  
1. 1 ÷ ½ means 1 divided by one‑half.  
2. A fraction’s reciprocal is obtained by swapping its numerator and denominator.  
3. The reciprocal of ½ is 2 ÷ 1 = 2.  
4. Multiply 1 by that reciprocal: 1 × 2 = 2.  
5. So, 1 ÷ ½ = 2.  

## Feedback  
Great job on most of your answers! You understood that dividing by a fraction is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal. Keep practicing this trick.

## Common Mistakes  
- Writing **½** instead of 2 – thinking the answer gets smaller.  
- Writing **4** – confusing “divide by a fraction” with “multiply by the fraction.”  
- Writing **3/2** – mixing up the steps of reciprocal and multiplication.

## Keep Practicing  
Try converting other fractions into their reciprocals and then multiplying. This will help you remember the shortcut for division by fractions.

## Question 19: When you multiply two proper fractions, the result is smaller than either fraction. (MULTIPLE_CHOICE)
**Correct Answer:** Correct  

**Explanation / Steps:**
1. A proper fraction is always between 0 and 1.  
2. Let the fractions be \(a\) and \(b\). Both \(a\) and \(b\) are less than 1.  
3. Multiply \(a\) by \(b\). Because \(b < 1\), the product \(a \times b\) is less than \(a \times 1\), which is just \(a\).  
4. Likewise, because \(a < 1\), the product \(a \times b\) is less than \(b \times 1\), which is \(b\).  
5. Therefore the result of multiplying two proper fractions is smaller than each of the original fractions.

## Feedback  
Excellent effort! You’ve seen that many of you understood the key idea. Keep up the good work!

## Common Mistakes  
1. **Thinking the product can be larger** – forgetting that multiplying by a number less than 1 always reduces the value.  
2. **Mixing up addition and multiplication** – sometimes you compare the product to the sum of the fractions instead of each fraction itself.  
3. **Not using the “< 1” property** – missing the fact that every proper fraction is less than 1.

## Keep Practicing  
Try multiplying different proper fractions (e.g., \( \frac{1}{3} \times \frac{1}{4} \)) and then compare the product with each factor. Remember: “anything times a number smaller than 1 gets smaller.”

## Question 48: How can you show that dividing a proper fraction by another proper fraction can give an answer greater than 1? (SHORT_ANSWER)
**Correct Answer:**  
If the divisor (the second fraction) is smaller than the dividend (the first fraction).

**Explanation / Steps:**
1. Rewrite the division as a multiplication by the reciprocal of the divisor.  
2. A proper fraction is less than 1. If that proper fraction is *smaller* than the dividend, its reciprocal is an improper fraction (value > 1).  
3. Multiply the dividend by this reciprocal.  
4. The result will be larger than 1 because you are multiplying by a number that is bigger than 1.  
5. Example: \( \frac{1}{2} \div \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{4}{1} = \frac{4}{2} = 2 > 1\).
