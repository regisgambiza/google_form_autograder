## Question 4: What is 5 ÷ 2? (MULTIPLE_CHOICE)
**Correct Answer:** 2.5  

**Explanation / Steps:**  
1. Write the division problem: 5 ÷ 2.  
2. 2 goes into 5 two times (2 × 2 = 4).  
3. Subtract 4 from 5 → remainder 1.  
4. Bring down a decimal point and add a 0 → 10.  
5. 2 goes into 10 five times (2 × 5 = 10).  
6. Combine the whole number 2 with the decimal part 0.5 → 2.5.  

## Feedback  
Great job on most of your answers! You understood how to divide whole numbers and use decimals. Keep up the good work!

## Common Mistakes  
1. **Wrong sign:** writing –2.5 instead of 2.5.  
2. **Wrong decimal:** writing 0.25 instead of 2.5.  

## Keep Practicing  
Try more division problems with different numbers, and remember to check the remainder first before adding the decimal part. You’ll get even better at it!

## Question 23: When dividing by a number less than 1, the result gets larger. (MULTIPLE_CHOICE)
**Correct Answer:**  
When you divide by a number that is less than 1, the answer becomes larger.

**Explanation / Steps:**
1. A number less than 1 is a fraction (e.g., 0.5 = ½).  
2. Dividing by a fraction is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal.  
3. The reciprocal of a number less than 1 is greater than 1 (reciprocal of ½ is 2).  
4. Multiplying by something bigger than 1 makes the result bigger.  
5. So dividing by a number < 1 gives a larger result.

## Feedback  
Great job! You’re beginning to see how fractions and reciprocals work together.

## Common Mistakes  
- Thinking that dividing by a small number makes the result smaller.  
- Forgetting that “less than 1” means a positive fraction, not a negative number.  
- Mixing up “divide by” with “multiply by”.

## Keep Practicing  
Try a few examples: divide 8 by 0.4 and 5 by 0.2. Notice how the answers grow larger. Good luck!

## Question 40: Explain the rules for signs when dividing integers. (SHORT_ANSWER)
**Correct Answer:**  
Negative × Negative = Positive  
Positive × Negative = Negative  

**Explanation / Steps**  
1. Look at the two signs.  
2. If the signs are the same (both + or both –) → the answer is **positive**.  
3. If the signs are different (one +, one –) → the answer is **negative**.  
4. Division uses the same rule because dividing is the same as multiplying by the reciprocal.  

## Feedback

## Common Mistakes  
- Writing **positive × positive = negative** instead of positive.  
- Forgetting that the rule is the same for division as for multiplication.
