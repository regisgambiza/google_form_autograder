## Question 38: Differentiate between average speed and instantaneous speed with an example. (SHORT_ANSWER)
**Correct Answer:**  
Average speed = total distance ÷ total time.  
Instantaneous speed = speed at one specific moment (for example, the reading on a car’s speedometer).

**Explanation / Steps:**  
1. Add up all the distance you travel.  
2. Add up all the time you spend moving.  
3. Divide the total distance by the total time → this is the **average speed**.  
4. To find **instantaneous speed**, look at the speed at one exact instant (e.g., the speedometer shows 70 km/h at a certain point).  
5. Example: If a car goes 100 km in 2 h, its average speed is 100 ÷ 2 = 50 km/h.  
   If the speedometer shows 70 km/h at some moment, that 70 km/h is the instantaneous speed.

## Feedback  
Great job! You understood that average speed measures overall motion, while instantaneous speed tells us how fast we are moving at a specific moment. Keep using clear examples to show the difference.

## Common Mistakes  
1. Mixing up “average speed” with “average speed per segment” (e.g., 10 km in 30 min = 20 km/h) instead of the whole trip.  
2. Forgetting that instantaneous speed is the speed at **one instant**, not an average over a period.  
3. Writing vague statements like “speed at a moment” without an example or explanation.

## Keep Practicing

## Keep Practicing  
Try making a small experiment: walk a set distance in 5‑minute intervals, write the times and distances, and then plot them. The more data you collect, the clearer your graph will look!
